## Overview

Placeholder content for Factorial project. This section will describe the company context, the team structure, and the scope of design work across the HR platform.

Factorial grew quickly across European markets, and the product surface area expanded faster than the design system could keep up. My role was to establish foundations that could scale with the product without slowing teams down.

## Challenge

Placeholder content. The core challenge was balancing speed of delivery with consistency across dozens of product squads, each shipping independently.

Teams were reinventing patterns for tables, forms, and navigation. Visual drift showed up in customer-facing flows and internal tools alike. Documentation existed but was easy to ignore when deadlines were tight.

We needed a system that was opinionated enough to reduce decisions, but flexible enough for edge cases in payroll, time tracking, and recruiting modules.

## Approach

Placeholder content. We started with an audit of the most common UI patterns in production, grouped by frequency and user impact.

From there we defined a token layer for color, spacing, and typography, then built a component library aligned to those tokens. Adoption was rolled out squad by squad, with office hours and paired design reviews rather than a big-bang migration.

Key principles: ship small, measure usage, deprecate aggressively, and keep Figma and code in sync through shared naming.

## Outcome

Placeholder content. Within two quarters, most squads had migrated core flows to the shared system. New feature work shipped faster because designers and engineers reused patterns instead of debating spacing.

Customer support tickets related to inconsistent UI behavior dropped. The system became the default starting point for new modules, including mobile and embedded experiences.
